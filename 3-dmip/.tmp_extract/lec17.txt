  Sources of Classification Error
• Errors may be caused by

  • Misidentification of parcels / Inaccurate selection of

    Training samples

  • Excessive generalization

  • Variations in detail of interpretation

  • Analyst’s experience
          Accuracy Assessments
• To determine the quality of the information derived from RS
  data through the classification process

• Purpose:
   • To validate the classification results , may be used for
     decision-making processes

   • End user must have a clear view of the quality of
     information before using it for any practical application
           Accuracy Assessments
• Compare two sources of information

   • Pixels or polygons from Classified RS image

   • Ground reference information (test data, a standard)

• If the Image classification results corresponds closely with test
  data, the processing is “Accurate” or of high accuracy

• In statistical terms , high accuracy means a low bias between
  the estimated/ classified and reference values
          Accuracy Assessments
• For accuracy assessment, the classified image and the reference

  data must be co-registered, such that both are at comparable

  level
           Accuracy Assessments
• Types:
   • Qualitative

   • Quantitative

• Qualitative assessments:
   – By comparing the classified image with some reference
     maps, high resolution aerial images or the ground truth data
     (generally visual)
                Assessment Types

• Quantitative assessments

   • To check the agreement between the classified and the

     reference data, to identify different types of classification

     errors
        Quantitative Assessments
• As the quantitative accuracy assessment relies upon the

  reference data, it is imperative that the reference data be highly

  accurate

• It is better to get fewer, but more accurate reference data which

  are truly representative of the classes under assessment
         Quantitative Assessments

•   Quantitative accuracy assessments can be performed as:


     • Non-site-specific assessments


     • Site-specific assessments

     • Error Matrix
          Quantitative Assessments
• Non-site-specific assessments

   – Does not consider agreement between the two maps at specific

     locations, but only the overall figures for the two maps (areas-based

     comparison)

   – Even though there may be close agreement between estimated class

     areas, the classification even then be inaccurate in terms of locational

     error, (no information about the locational accuracy)
Non-site-specific assessments
         Quantitative Assessments

• Site-specific assessments


   – Assessment of the agreement between the classified and


     reference maps at specific locations

                                 Sampling
• What sampling scheme should be used?

• How many samples should be collected?

•   Random Samples: is best with a large enough sample size,

     – However, as sample size decreases, categories that are less prevalent on the

       landscape can be missed using a simple random sample

• Stratified random sample: based on class categories

• “Sufficient” sample size? ----50 –100 samples per category as a general

    guideline
              Confusion/Error Matrices

• Confusion/error matrices ( site specific )

   • Accepted as standards for evaluating and reporting the accuracy of

      classification results

   • Are also known as Error or Confusion or Contingency matrices

      because they identify the errors as well as the misclassifications and

      confusion among different classes
      Accuracy Assessment-Error Matrix
•   Comparison is done by creating an error matrix from which different

    accuracy measures can be calculated

• Error matrix compares on class-by-class basis the relationship between

    known reference data (ground truth) and corresponding results of

    automated classification

• Matrix is square, no of rows equal to number of classes

                                  Kappa
•   Another measure of statistical classification accuracy can be performed using
    the kappa (κ hat) index of agreement




•   r:      number of land cover classes (rows)
•   N:      total number of test pixels included in the error matrix
•   ∑xii total of diagonal elements
•   xi+ : total in row i
•   x+i: total in column i of the matrix elements
•   Kappa values are typically between 0 and 1, an indication that the observed
    classification is that much percent better than the results from chance
    assignment
•   Considers the possibility of the agreement occurring by chance
