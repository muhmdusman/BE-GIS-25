                              LiDAR
• LiDAR is an acronym derived from Light Detection and Ranging

• Also known as LADAR (LASER Detection and Ranging), and also as

   LASER Altimetry

• LiDARs are based on an application of lasers, they use light that is

   composed of a very narrow band of wavelengths

• Such light can be transmitted over large distances as a narrow

   beam that will diverge only slightly, in contrast with most light in

   our everyday experience
                              LiDAR
• LiDAR is an active sensor

• LiDAR uses electromagnetic energy in the visible and near infrared

  wavelengths

• LiDAR is a nadir-looking instrument

• As LiDAR operates in the visible and near-infrared wavelengths, its

  signal is affected by atmospheric conditions

• LiDAR records information at discrete points across the swath
                                LiDAR
• LiDAR instrument measures the time taken by an energy pulse to reach
  the ground, and for a part of the scattered radiation to return to the
  sensor

• Higher the pulse rate more the swath/coverage but weaker the returns,

       ( few hundred to about 30,000 per second)

• A single pulse may return multiple returns

  technological developments

   – Improved electro-mechanical technology, sensors and lasers can
      be accurately and reliably controlled in moving aircrafts

   – Precise aircraft positioning and orientation using GPS , Inertial

                 Important Components
• Inertial measurement units (IMUs): enable precise control and

    recording of orientation of aircraft (roll, pitch, and yaw)

•   GPS: provides accurate records of geographic location of an aircraft

    as it acquires data

• Highly accurate clocks: permits precise timing of LiDAR pulses

    required to create high performance LiDAR scanning systems
                                   LiDAR
•   LiDAR measures the distance from the sensor to the ground
• As the position of the instrument is known, accurate position of the each
    return pulse can be calculated, and the distances can be converted to
    elevations above a specified geodetic datum
• Raster map of these elevations can be generated by interpolation
• Set of produced elevation values does not necessarily define a digital elevation
    model (DEM), because the LiDAR pulse is reflected back by the first object of
    sufficient size and density that it meets as it travels downwards from the
    instrument
• These objects may be a branch, a tree crown, the ground surface, or the top of
    a building, depending on the area being viewed
                                    LiDAR
• Term 'first return’ or ‘first bounce’ system records the time taken between the
   emission of the pulse of light energy and the reception at the sensor of the
   first return

• while the second is the position of the last indication of backscatter. This
   second event is called the last return

• There is no difference between the first and last return if the target object
   does not transmit light

• if it does, then the time difference between the first and last bounce is
   proportional to the height of the object above the ground

                                  LiDAR
• Set of point elevation values can be used to generate a digital surface model

   (DSM), which shows the elevation of the highest reflective object on the ground

• LiDAR data can be used to map the highest point on a building, or of a tree

• To generate a DEM from the DSM, the surface objects must be removed, or the

   height of the superimposed object must be estimated

• DSM is useful in itself

• LiDAR images can be used to generate 3D urban models

                     Digital Elevation Model
•   Reliability with which the Earth surface can be represented is determined by
    horizontal spacing and vertical accuracy of the elevation
•   Sudden elevation change (such as cliffs or across drainage features) cannot be
    represented well by gridded DEMs
•   Interpolation on either sides is misleading, since the slopes at such points are not
    continuous
•   Some gridded DEM includes break lines which indicates the discontinuities in the
    topographic surface
•   A break line is a line of terrain where there is a significant change in slope. Break
    lines fall on ridge lines, valleys, ditches and other natural or artificial features
    where there is a change in slope
•   When interpolating elevations from DEM in the vicinity of break line, only points
    on the same side of the break line are used

                Advantages & Applications
• Quick data delivery (DEM and DSM) with in 24 hours of data
  acquisition
• Can operate at night
• Convenience and flexibility in flight planning

   – Designing wireless communication systems in urban regions
