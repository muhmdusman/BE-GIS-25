# Lecture 1: Remote Sensing — An Overview

---

## 1. What is Remote Sensing?

- **Remote** = Not in contact with / Away from
- **Sensing** = The act of observation
- **Remote Sensing (RS)** = Acquiring information about an object **without being in physical contact** with it
- Our **eye** is the simplest example of a remote sensor — seeing is remote sensing

---

## 2. Four Key Functions of Remote Sensing (The 4 M's)

| Function       | Meaning |
|----------------|---------|
| **Mapping**    | Collecting thematic and quantitative baseline data |
| **Measuring**  | A more rigorous mapping process — quantifying and documenting attributes |
| **Modelling**  | Describing a system through **precise mathematical relations** of inputs/outputs to simulate present, past, or future behavior |
| **Monitoring** | Regular assessment of conditions by recording changes in natural phenomena and human activities |

> All four functions need **valid, up-to-date, spatially and temporally explicit** natural resource data for decision-making.

---

## 4. Data vs Information

- **Data** = Raw representation that can be operated upon
- **Information** = Data that has been **interpreted by human beings**
- Data once interpreted → becomes Information

---

## 5. Geospatial Data Sources

There are **four** main geospatial data sources:

1. **Field / Ground Surveys**
2. **Photography** (Ground and Aerial)
3. **Remote Sensing** (Imaging — Space and Airborne)
4. **Positioning Systems** (e.g., GPS)

---

## 6. Principle of Remote Sensing

- A **remote sensing satellite** is an artificial satellite designed to observe Earth from orbit
- RS mainly concerns the measurement of **Electromagnetic (EM) energy** which is:
  - **Reflected**
  - **Scattered**
  - **Emitted** by different Earth surface objects
- Different objects return **different kinds and amounts** of energy → detecting these differences enables **identification**

---

## 7. Remote Sensing Process (7 Steps)

1. **Energy Source / Illumination** (e.g., the Sun)
2. **Radiation and the Atmosphere** (energy passes through atmosphere)
3. **Radiation's Interaction with the Target** (Earth surface)
4. **Recording of Energy by the Sensor**
5. **Transmission, Reception, and Processing**
6. **Interpretation and Analysis**
7. **Application**

---

## 8. Earth Observation System — Components

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Scene** | Earth's surface + Atmosphere |
| **RS Systems** | Sensors + Platforms |
| **Processing/Analysis** | Converting data to information |

- **Remote Sensors** = Detection and recording instruments for RS
- Carried on **aircraft** and **earth-orbiting spacecraft** → "Eye in the sky"

---

## 9. Why Specialized Knowledge is Needed for RS Data Analysis

- Unusual **image presentation**
- Unusual **scales and resolutions**
- Unfamiliar **overhead (top-down) view**
- Use of **several regions of EM spectrum** (beyond visible)

---

## 10. Advantages of Remote Sensing

1. **Large area coverage** — enables global/regional surveys
2. **Repetitive coverage** — allows monitoring of dynamic themes (water, agriculture)
3. **Data over inaccessible areas** (e.g., Antarctica, deep forests)
4. **Multiple heights** — different scales and resolutions
5. **Fast and accurate** data processing

---

## 11. Limitations of Remote Sensing

1. Often **oversold** — cannot provide all information needed
2. **Human interaction** introduces errors (selection, processing)
3. Instruments may become **uncalibrated** → errors in data
4. Active RS systems may **affect the phenomenon** being investigated
5. RS data may be **expensive** to collect
6. **People who analyze** data are often the most expensive part

---

## 12. Earth Resource Information

- Any information about: **vegetation, soils, minerals, rocks, water, atmospheric characteristics, and urban infrastructure**

---

## 13. Requirements for Successful RS Application

1. A **clear definition** of the problem
2. **Evaluation** of potential for addressing it with RS
3. Identification of appropriate **data acquisition procedures**
4. Determination of **data interpretation technique**
5. Need for **other reference data**
6. Criteria to **judge quality** of collected information

---

## 14. Applications of Remote Sensing

- Regional planning and site investigations
- Water resource monitoring (quality, quantity, distribution, floods, pollution)
- Highway alignment
- Terrain mapping
- Urban and regional planning (population, housing, traffic, urban sprawl)
- Environmental monitoring
- Meteorology
- Mineral resource exploration
- Defence and security
- Flood and drought monitoring
- Forest fire detection
- Deforestation tracking
- Vegetation analysis
- Natural disaster management (wildfires, floods, storms, volcanic eruptions, earthquakes, hurricanes)
