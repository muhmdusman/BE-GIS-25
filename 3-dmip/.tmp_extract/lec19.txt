              Urban Heat Island (UHI)
• An urban heat island (UHI) is a metropolitan area that is
  significantly warmer than its surrounding rural areas
  due to human activities

• The term "heat island" describes built up areas that are
  hotter than nearby rural areas
              Urban Heat Island (UHI)

• Urban materials such as construction material, roofs, asphalt,

  concrete and roads absorb more heat from the sun

• The subsequent release of the energy causes urban areas to

  be warmer compared to the surrounding non-urban areas

  giving rise to the urban heat effect
              Urban Heat Island (UHI)
• Depending on the location, the urban heat island effect can
  be aggravated by the absence or removal of vegetation, which
  provides shade and evapotranspiration to cool the air on
  warm days
                 Urban Heat Island (UHI)
• The dynamics of the heat island effect are a function of the
   – Time

   – Meteorological conditions

   – Local and urban characteristics

       and consequently,

• These functions could be unique for particular urban areas

• In general, parks, lakes and open areas, appear relatively cooler
  compared to commercial, industrial or dense buildings
               Urban Heat Island (UHI)
• Traditionally, urban heat island effects are observed from the

  measurement of air temperatures from fixed weather

  observation stations and sensors mounted on moving vehicles
        Urban Heat Island (UHI) Effect
• Steps to reduce the heat island effect
   • Increasing tree and vegetative cover

   • Creating green roofs (also called "rooftop gardens“

   • Installing cool—mainly reflective roofs

   • Using cool pavements (permeable pavement parking lot
     that includes shade trees)
        Urban Heat Island (UHI) Effect
• During mornings, urban materials will slowly absorb heat

• Urban heat islands are best observed in the night when
  maximum radiation from urban materials occurs

• The timing for satellites is therefore not optimized for
  detecting urban heat islands

         Land Surface Temperature (LST)
• Land Surface Temperature (LST) is defined as:
   – The temperature of the interface between the Earth’s surface
     and its atmosphere
   – The temperature of the air near the Earth´s surface
   – The skin temperature of the ground
• The LST in remote sensing can be defined as:

   – The average surface temperature of the ground under the
     pixel scale(pixel size) mixed with different fractions of surface
     types
        Land Surface Temperature (LST)
• LSTs are regarded as a function of four surface and subsurface
  properties:

   – Reflectance/ Albedo

   – Emissivity

   – Thermal properties of urban construction materials
     (including moisture), and

   – The composition and structure of the urban canopy
         Land Surface Temperature (LST)
• LST: an important parameter in understanding the urban

  thermal environment and its dynamics

• Due to the strong heterogeneity of land surface characteristics

  such as vegetation, topography, and soil, LST changes rapidly in

  space as well as in time
          Land Surface Temperature (LST)
• LST is widely used in a variety of fields including

   • Evapotranspiration

   • Climate change

   • Hydrological cycle

   • Vegetation monitoring

   • Urban climate and environmental studies
          Land Surface Temperature (LST)
• Given the complexity of surface temperature over land, ground
  measurements cannot practically provide values over wide
  areas

• With the development of remote sensing from space, satellite
  data offer the only possibility for measuring LST over the entire
  globe with sufficiently high temporal resolution and with
  complete spatially averaged rather than point values
         Land Surface Temperature (LST)
• Direct estimation of LST from the radiation emitted in the TIR

  spectral region is difficult to perform with accuracy, since the

  radiances measured by the radiometers onboard satellites

  depend not only on surface parameters (temperature and

  emissivity) but also on atmospheric effects
         Land Surface Temperature (LST)
• Surface temperature is defined using the radiance emitted by a

  surface, this temperature also known as the skin temperature




• Brightness temperature is the land surface temperature

  obtained on the satellite level by thermal infrared (TIR) sensors
              Thermal IR Remote Sensing

• Thermal infrared radiation refers to electromagnetic energy within a

   wavelengths of between 3 and 14 micrometers

• Most remote sensing applications make use of the 3 to 5 and 8 to 14

   micrometer range, atmospheric windows      (due to minimum absorption)


• Systems on aircraft flying below the ozone layer are not affected and

   record the full 8-14 µm band
